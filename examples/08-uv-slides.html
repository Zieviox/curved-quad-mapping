UV Slides

  1. Option (b): each side arc of a piece is projected onto the screen, where it is a 2D conic K. Then u = K3 / (K3 + K1) and v = K0 / (K0 + K2). Each K is scaled to 1 at the piece's projected centre, so the centre gets (0.5, 0.5).
  2. Two forms of K. The projected arc is part of a whole ellipse. The full conic K is also 0 on the ellipse's other half, which can run back across the piece (smooth cube): then u drops to 0 in the wrong place. The arc only form is 0 on the arc alone, at the price of a square root per side.
  3. Left: what the camera sees, coloured by that screen (u, v). Right: the same quad from a second camera that stays put. Its surface is painted with the left camera's screen (u, v). The thin lines are the surface's own (u, v) from step 11.
  4. Drag the left view. If the checker on the right moves across the thin lines, the (u, v) is sliding on the surface.
  5. ● the surface point at the marker (u, v). ● where the left camera's screen (u, v) says the marker is. The dotted path is the red dot for 25 camera angles around the quad.

How far it slides

Slide = for a surface point the left camera sees, the distance between that point and the surface point its screen (u, v) names, in % of the average edge length. Only parts facing the left camera are counted.
