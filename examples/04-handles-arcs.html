Handles and Arcs

  1. Every quad corner has a handle: its face-corner normal, not normalized. The direction sets the tangent, and the length sets how far the curve bulges.
  2. Between two handles runs one ellipse arc (a conic). If the arc would curve back (S) or twist out of a plane, an artificial handle splits it into two arcs.
  3. Sharp edges (different normals on each side) get rounded inside each quad by the blending distance, like a bevel. Each quad computes its own pieces, reading its neighbours' normals only so that shared borders agree.
  4. Only the borders are shown here. Filling the pieces comes next (step 9b).
