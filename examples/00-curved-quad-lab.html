Curved Quad Surface Lab

Nagata, Phong, PN-quad and ellipsoid (quadric) surfaces built from corner positions and vertex normals, compared with Catmull-Clark (Blender's Subdivision Surface). Includes a displacement-map test and per-operation cycle costs for the pixel-stepping pipeline.

Shape and displacement test

Target = Catmull-Clark level 4 plus a stand-in baked map (2×2 bumps per quad, zero along quad edges) pushed along the subdivided surface's normal. Map as baked applies that scalar map to each base along its own normal. Converted adds the vector map (target − base at the same UV). Error = distance to the target as % of the average cage edge. Ellipsoid quad: pixel-corner cost

A different pipeline from the step walk above. Each pixel corner is a ray from the camera. It hits the piece's quadric (quadratic), then the ray from the quadric's centre through that hit meets the flat bilinear base (quadratic), which gives (u, v). Neighbouring pixels share corners, so this costs about one corner per pixel. Counts are for one piece; the S-split adds pieces. Cycles = throughput on ports 0 and 1 (FP ops 0.5, COMISS and DIV/SQRT issue 1 each); the divider runs in parallel.

Diagnosis

Nagata quad

  • Cheapest build, transform and row. Hot loop ties Phong.
  • Quadratic edges: no S-bends.
  • Build divides by 1 − (n0·n1)². Blows up when normals are parallel but the edge isn't flat; needs a threshold and fallback branch.

Phong quad

  • No divisions, no singular cases, branch-free build.
  • Bulges only. Goes straight where both normals are parallel.
  • Curvature amount α is a tuning value.

PN-quad

  • Cubic edges: makes S-bends.
  • Step +6 cycles (2 walkers) / +4 (1 walker); rows and transform about 2×.
  • Interior points not uniquely defined; counts use the zero-twist rule.

Ellipsoid quad (quadric)

  • Pixel corners in closed form: ray vs quadric (quadratic), then (u, v) by ray from the center vs the bilinear base (quadratic).
  • Exact through the 4 corners; normals are a least-squares fit.
  • Corners and normals alone leave a whole family of exact fits, including two flat planes. A shoulder point (PN-quad midpoint) picks the curved one.
  • 4 corners + 8 normal conditions = 12, but a quadric has 9: normals are a least-squares fit otherwise.
  • One quadric per quad: neighbours only meet along edges if they share the quadric. Conic edges: no S-bends.

Ellipsoid quad + S-split

  • Makes S-bends: where an edge's PN cubic inflects, an artificial point (position + normal from that edge only) splits the quad, and each side gets its own quadric.
  • Wave: all 16 pieces match their corner normals exactly (0.0°).
  • Each corner is tried on up to 2 pieces (4 for a 4-way split). Build cost per piece.
  • Pieces meet only at their corners: creases along split lines and quad edges.

Neighbour trim (option A)

  • Exact border by construction: a piece ends where its quadric crosses its neighbour's (union on convex edges, intersection on concave).
  • Fails here: neighbours that match the shared normals are tangent at the shared corners, so they touch there instead of crossing. Between the corners one usually stays above the other, so there's no crossing line along the edge. Result: holes and ribbons.
  • A quadric is a whole closed (or infinite) shape. Its far side must be masked off by mapping each test point back into the neighbour's patch.
  • Per pixel corner near an edge: up to 2 neighbour tests at 31 cycles each.

Ellipsoid pieces (handles & arcs)

  • Current method (examples 04–05): face-corner normals as handles, ellipse arcs between them, rounded sharp edges (blending 3%), one quadric per piece, split depth 1.
  • Shared borders are identical curves, so where a piece's arcs lie on its quadric there is no crack (cube, wave).
  • Pieces don't share the quad's (u, v), so this view's error is the distance to the nearest surface point, not the same-UV distance the other views use.
  • Where a piece's 4 arcs can't share one quadric, the crack stays (two-point rule).

Catmull-Clark

  • Smooth across quad borders. Regular quads are exact bicubic B-splines at PN-quad hot-loop cost; no normals needed.
  • Doesn't pass through the vertices; shrinks.
  • Needs neighbors. Extraordinary vertices and boundaries need other rules.

None of the normal-based surfaces is smooth across patch borders. Displacement maps only match the surface they were baked against; converting to a vector map relative to the flat quad is exact and removes the curved base from the hot loop at 3× map memory.
