Curved Quad Surface Lab

Nagata, Phong and PN-quad surfaces built from corner positions and vertex normals, compared with Catmull-Clark (Blender's Subdivision Surface). Includes a displacement-map test and per-operation cycle costs for the pixel-stepping pipeline.

Shape and displacement test

Target = Catmull-Clark level 4 plus a stand-in baked map (2×2 bumps per quad, zero along quad edges) pushed along the subdivided surface's normal. Map as baked applies that scalar map to each base along its own normal. Converted adds the vector map (target − base at the same UV). Error = distance to the target as % of the average cage edge. Rows don't fit in registers, so each step re-reads 18 floats from L1 (24 for bicubic). Those loads don't depend on u and run on separate ports.

Loop-carried chain (sets the 1-walker cost)

Z, Z_u 8 (bicubic 12) → X_u·Z 4 → FMA 4 → square 4 → FMA 4 → RSQRT 4 → du 4 → u += du 4 = 36 (bicubic 40)

Throughput sum is 26 cycles (bicubic 32). Interleaving 2 walkers (two rows or two patches) hides the chain.

Diagnosis

Nagata quad

  • Cheapest build, transform and row. Hot loop ties Phong.
  • Quadratic edges: no S-bends.
  • Build divides by 1 − (n0·n1)². Blows up when normals are parallel but the edge isn't flat; needs a threshold and fallback branch.

Phong quad

  • No divisions, no singular cases, branch-free build.
  • Bulges only. Goes straight where both normals are parallel.
  • Curvature amount α is a tuning value.

PN-quad

  • Cubic edges: makes S-bends.
  • Step +6 cycles (2 walkers) / +4 (1 walker); rows and transform about 2×.
  • Interior points not uniquely defined; counts use the zero-twist rule.

Catmull-Clark

  • Smooth across quad borders. Regular quads are exact bicubic B-splines at PN-quad hot-loop cost; no normals needed.
  • Doesn't pass through the vertices; shrinks.
  • Needs neighbors. Extraordinary vertices and boundaries need other rules.

None of the normal-based surfaces is smooth across patch borders. Displacement maps only match the surface they were baked against; converting to a vector map relative to the flat quad is exact and removes the curved base from the hot loop at 3× map memory.
