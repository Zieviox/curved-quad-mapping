Curved quads · step 14 · build, then resolve

Two-Pass Map

  1. Pass 1, build (per piece). Project the piece's 4 edge arcs. On each pixel row, where an edge crosses the row is one quadratic (straight edge: linear). The sorted crossings are the spans this piece covers on that row. The piece never looks at any other pixel.
  2. Along a span, for each pixel: u = K3/(K3+K1), v = K0/(K0+K2) from the edges (stepped along the row), then the 3D point P(u, v) on the piece's surface (one quadratic), then its distance from the camera. The piece writes (piece, u, v, distance) into that pixel's list.
  3. Pass 2, resolve (per pixel). Go over each pixel's list once; the closest entry wins. Its numbers go into the final arrays (piece, u, v, distance, occlusion map), and the pixel is coloured from them.
  4. Every step is a formula evaluated once. Tap a pixel to see the numbers it holds.

Skylake float32 model, one core, limited by throughput: ADD/MUL/FMA/MAX/MIN 0.5 cycle each, DIV and SQRT 3 (one divider), compares 1; each part's cycles are the largest of the three. FMA counts as 2 FLOPs. Per pixel = the part's cycles over all screen pixels. P(u, v) is counted from the step 10 forward map as written: 97 FMA, 86 MUL, 33 ADD, 8 DIV, 2 SQRT, 4 CMP (half of it is picking the right root against the blend of the 4 edge arcs).
