R&D · CPU-only, closed form

Curved Quad Mapping

Mapping curved quad patches to screen pixels, (quad, u, v) ↔ (pixel x, y), on the CPU with no frame buffer, for a spatial occlusion system. The final prototype will be in Odin. These pages are the experiments, in the order they were done. Every page runs in a browser, including on a phone.

    Surface lab

    Which curved surface do we build from 4 corners + 4 normals?

    • Nagata, Phong, PN-quad and Catmull-Clark compared on a cube and a wave, with a displacement-map test.
    • A displacement map converted to a vector map relative to the flat quad is exact.

  2. 1

    Derivative-step walker dropped

    Can we walk each patch in steps of one projected pixel?

    • One-pixel steps leave gaps: 2.54% on the cube at 256².
    • Safety 0.7 removes the interior gaps but nearly doubles the work: 2.92 pairs per pixel, about 76 cycles per pixel.
    • The step is a local guess. Dropped in favour of an exact mechanism.
    Open the walk test
  3. 2

    Exact crossings along a row

    Where exactly does a row of the patch cross into the next pixel?

    • A pixel edge is a plane through the camera. Crossing it is a quadratic for Nagata and Phong.
    • Exact along a row, but rows are 1D: choosing them is still a guess.
    Open the row explainer
  4. 3

    Pixel grid as knives

    Start from the pixels instead: what does each pixel own on the patch?

    • Each pixel edge slices the patch. Each slice is closed form.
    • Pixel corners (two slices meeting) are degree 8 on Phong and Nagata, so no closed form. They are a quadratic on a quadric (ellipsoid) or a bilinear patch.
    Open the slices page
  5. 4

    Ellipsoid quads

    Can each quad be a piece of a quadric, built from its corners and normals?

    • Fit through the 4 corners, with normals by least squares. A pixel corner maps to (u, v) with two quadratics.
    • A quadric has 9 free numbers but corners + normals ask for 12, so normals are only exact when they agree with one quadric.
    Open the lab (Ellipsoid view)
  6. 5

    S-curves by splitting

    How do we get S-bends from quadrics, which can't inflect?

    • Add an artificial point where an edge's cubic inflects, split there, and give each side its own quadric. The wave gets a real S with every normal exact.
    • Pixel corner: 29 cycles, or 39 if latency-bound, per piece.
    • Open: pieces crease where they meet. Next: share data with neighbours.
    Open the lab (S-split view)
  7. 6

    Neighbour trim failed

    Can neighbouring pieces end exactly where their shapes cross?

    • Exact in theory, but neighbours that match the shared normals touch at the corners instead of crossing, so there's no crossing line along the edge. Holes and ribbons.
    • Kept: a neighbour's shape only counts inside the neighbour's own patch area.
    • Next: shared edge curves, which need more pieces per quad.
    Open the lab (trim view)
  8. 7

    Literature: shared edge curves

    How does published work make quadric pieces meet exactly?

    • Two conics share one quadric only if they meet twice (Cayley), so one quadric per quad can't hold 4 edge curves.
    • Known methods split faces into many pieces. Set aside in favour of our own handle design.
  9. 8–9

    Handles and arcs

    Normals as handles (direction = tangent, length = bulge), one ellipse arc between every two handles, sharp edges rounded inside each quad.

    • Shared borders match exactly on every edge.
    • Hard cube → an exact rounded box. Smooth cube → arcs exactly on the sphere.
    • S-curves and twists split at an artificial handle, with 0 failures.
    • Pieces filled with one quadric each: rounded box, sphere and wave close to the measurement floor (wave 0.0025%). The mixed cube's side pieces miss by 36%: their 4 arcs don't fit one quadric. Open.
    Open handles and arcs
  10. 10

    Clean-slate conversion

    Same math, rebuilt conversion (lookups → vertex rounding → shared arc cache → pieces → splits), plus "split further".

    • Cube, hard cube and wave: closed. Smooth cube at length × 2: edges closed now, insides still have holes.
    • Mixed cube: splitting once halves the crack (8.35% → 3.44%); deeper splits leave holes. Pieces whose corner arcs can't share a quadric only get smaller, never exact.
    Open quad pieces
  11. 11

    Pixel corners current

    Map every pixel corner to (piece, u, v): ray → piece's quadric (one quadratic) → its 4 side planes.

    • Smooth cube, rounded box, wave: 0 holes over 12 camera views.
    • Mixed cube: 14 holes in 19,252 corners. Its cracks, as the pixels see them.
    • About 12–34 cycles per pixel (15 per tested piece, 0.8–2.2 pieces tested).
    • Occlusion map: each pixel keeps its nearest normalized distance (0 = empty), with a farthest-of-4 mip chain.
    Open pixel corners

The cost model throughout is Intel Skylake, float32 scalar: ADD, MUL and FMA have latency 4 and throughput 0.5 cycles; SQRT 12/3; DIV 11/3. Pages compute in JavaScript float64.
