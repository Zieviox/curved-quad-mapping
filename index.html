R&D · CPU-only, closed form

Curved Quad Mapping

Mapping curved quad patches to screen pixels, (quad, u, v) ↔ (pixel x, y), on the CPU with no frame buffer, for a spatial occlusion system. The final prototype will be in Odin. These pages are the experiments, in the order they were done. Every page runs in a browser, including on a phone.

    Surface lab

    Which curved surface do we build from 4 corners + 4 normals?

    • Nagata, Phong, PN-quad and Catmull-Clark compared on a cube and a wave, with a displacement-map test.
    • A displacement map converted to a vector map relative to the flat quad is exact.

  2. 1

    Derivative-step walker dropped

    Can we walk each patch in steps of one projected pixel?

    • One-pixel steps leave gaps: 2.54% on the cube at 256².
    • Safety 0.7 removes the interior gaps but nearly doubles the work: 2.92 pairs per pixel, about 76 cycles per pixel.
    • The step is a local guess. Dropped in favour of an exact mechanism.
    Open the walk test
  3. 2

    Exact crossings along a row

    Where exactly does a row of the patch cross into the next pixel?

    • A pixel edge is a plane through the camera. Crossing it is a quadratic for Nagata and Phong.
    • Exact along a row, but rows are 1D: choosing them is still a guess.
    Open the row explainer
  4. 3

    Pixel grid as knives

    Start from the pixels instead: what does each pixel own on the patch?

    • Each pixel edge slices the patch. Each slice is closed form.
    • Pixel corners (two slices meeting) are degree 8 on Phong and Nagata, so no closed form. They are a quadratic on a quadric (ellipsoid) or a bilinear patch.
    Open the slices page
  5. 4

    Ellipsoid quads

    Can each quad be a piece of a quadric, built from its corners and normals?

    • Fit through the 4 corners, with normals by least squares. A pixel corner maps to (u, v) with two quadratics.
    • A quadric has 9 free numbers but corners + normals ask for 12, so normals are only exact when they agree with one quadric.
    Open the lab (Ellipsoid view)
  6. 5

    S-curves by splitting

    How do we get S-bends from quadrics, which can't inflect?

    • Add an artificial point where an edge's cubic inflects, split there, and give each side its own quadric. The wave gets a real S with every normal exact.
    • Pixel corner: 29 cycles, or 39 if latency-bound, per piece.
    • Open: pieces crease where they meet. Next: share data with neighbours.
    Open the lab (S-split view)
  7. 6

    Neighbour trim failed

    Can neighbouring pieces end exactly where their shapes cross?

    • Exact in theory, but neighbours that match the shared normals touch at the corners instead of crossing, so there's no crossing line along the edge. Holes and ribbons.
    • Kept: a neighbour's shape only counts inside the neighbour's own patch area.
    • Next: shared edge curves, which need more pieces per quad.
    Open the lab (trim view)
  8. 7

    Literature: shared edge curves

    How does published work make quadric pieces meet exactly?

    • Two conics share one quadric only if they meet twice (Cayley), so one quadric per quad can't hold 4 edge curves.
    • Known methods split faces into many pieces. Set aside in favour of our own handle design.
  9. 8–9

    Handles and arcs

    Normals as handles (direction = tangent, length = bulge), one ellipse arc between every two handles, sharp edges rounded inside each quad.

    • Shared borders match exactly on every edge.
    • Hard cube → an exact rounded box. Smooth cube → arcs exactly on the sphere.
    • S-curves and twists split at an artificial handle, with 0 failures.
    • Pieces filled with one quadric each: rounded box, sphere and wave close to the measurement floor (wave 0.0025%). The mixed cube's side pieces miss by 36%: their 4 arcs don't fit one quadric. Open.
    Open handles and arcs
  10. 10

    Clean-slate conversion

    Same math, rebuilt conversion (lookups → vertex rounding → shared arc cache → pieces → splits), plus "split further".

    • Cube, hard cube and wave: closed. Smooth cube at length × 2: edges closed now, insides still have holes.
    • Mixed cube: splitting once halves the crack (8.35% → 3.44%); deeper splits leave holes. Pieces whose corner arcs can't share a quadric only get smaller, never exact.
    Open quad pieces
  11. 11

    Pixel corners

    Map every pixel corner to (piece, u, v): ray → piece's quadric (one quadratic) → its 4 side planes.

    • Smooth cube, rounded box, wave: 0 holes over 12 camera views.
    • Mixed cube: 14 holes in 19,252 corners. Its cracks, as the pixels see them.
    • About 12–34 cycles per pixel (15 per tested piece, 0.8–2.2 pieces tested).
    • Occlusion map: each pixel keeps its nearest normalized distance (0 = empty), with a farthest-of-4 mip chain.
    Open pixel corners
  12. 12

    Open problems, explained

    Why do some pieces crack, why do some have no surface inside, and why can a hit land on the far side?

    • Cracks: neighbours miss their shared arc differently (8.35% vs 0.37%).
    • Length × 2: the fitted shape splits into two branches and the piece's middle falls between them.
    • Far side: the region between a piece's side planes widens with depth. A cheaper sign rule is the next thing to test.
    Open the explanations
  13. 13

    UV slides

    Option (b): take (u, v) from the projected side arcs only. Does it stay on the same surface point when the camera moves?

    • Flat pieces: exact. Curved pieces slide: smooth cube 17.6% of an edge at the marker, 12.8% even straight on; wave 3.25%.
    • The full projected conic also vanishes on the ellipse's other half, which crosses the smooth cube's face. Only an arc-only form (one square root per side) works.
    • Folds: past a silhouette, the projected side runs across visible surface and its (u, v) leaves 0…1.
    • Whole mesh with the depth test: the same piece as step 11 on 100% of the wave, 99.3% of the rounded box and 53.7% of the smooth cube, where every face folds. FPS counter and cycle/FLOP table on the page.
    Open UV slides
  14. 14

    Two-pass map

    Pass 1: each piece walks only its own spans (edge crossings per row, one quadratic each) and writes (piece, u, v, distance) into each pixel's list. Pass 2: each pixel keeps its closest entry.

    • About 15–111 cycles per pixel in the model (11% of a 1,000-cycle budget on the smooth cube). P(u, v) is 108 of the 124 cycles per written pixel.
    • Same piece as step 11 on 98–100% of the wave and 99.3–99.6% of the rounded box.
    • A piece that folds past the outline fails as a whole: its far edge projects inside its own image. Smooth cube 80–87%.
    Open the two-pass map
  15. 15

    Closed-form depth only

    Only the closed form (edge crossings, u and v, P(u, v), distance) written as plain arithmetic; keeps the nearest depth per pixel and prints the depth array.

    • Same depth as step 14 on every pixel.
    • Smooth cube 5.4 ms per 200² frame in the browser, against 43 ms in step 14: the time was going into allocation, not arithmetic.

  16. 16

    Operation stress test

    Every solving and detection method on real pieces: measured time, counted operations and Skylake cycles, and accuracy in float64 and float32 against an independent answer key.

    • Ray × ellipsoid + side planes: exact, 23 model cycles. Edge-based u, v: slide 38% of an edge on average. P(u, v): 111 cycles.
    • Step 11's forward-map check fails in float32 (rejects every hard-cube pixel); the sign rule fails on pieces that turn away partway; nearest-in-planes takes the far side on the wave.
    • Packed layout (one flat array, stepped inside spans, 10-byte output): 4–6× faster with identical results; ray + sign rule 16 model cycles per pixel.
    • Per-pixel facing picks the right hit everywhere except 430 no-hit pixels on the mixed cube (14 packed cycles). Pictures: what remains wrong is coverage from the edge spans (outline bulges, seams), the same for every rule.
    • Option 1, bulge box (closed form from the quadric's outline points): misses no pixel of any piece; about 12 cycles per pixel walked. Left: back pieces tie with front ones on a shared sphere; a seam band on the wave.

    Quick test: edge marks

    Only the edge ellipses exist. Can a pixel corner's u, v in the middle of a quad be worked out from where its two knives cross the edges?

    • Flat faces: exact. All quadratics, 10–73 ms per frame in the browser.
    • A ball is cut off at each face's edges: edge marks alone can't know how far a face bulges past them.

  21. 21

    Test: ellipses inside the knives

    A knife marks two edges; each mark inherits a normal; the two marks and their normals give a new ellipse across the quad; crossing knives cut it at the pixel corners.

    • Smooth cube: every point lies on the exact ball to 0.0005% of the radius. Flat faces exact. All quadratics, 5–25 ms per knife direction in the browser.
    • Rows alone miss the bulge above and below the edges; columns alone miss it at the sides. Both together close the outline but disagree on u, v (speckle).
    Open knife ellipses
  22. 23

    Test: two ellipses meet current

    Each pixel corner gets a row ellipse and a column ellipse; the corner is a blend of the two, each counting most near its own marks.

    • Smooth cube and hard cube: the two meet exactly; ball exact to 0.0005% of the radius.
    • Mixed cube and wave: the two pass more than 2% of the radius apart over large areas (painted red). The blend there averages two different shapes.
